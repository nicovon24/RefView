# Fictional Community interactions v1

## Scope and exposure

Two repository-authored match fixtures are explicitly fictional. Their names, teams, dates, incidents, and referee decisions are invented. Ratings and comments are real owner contributions about that fiction, never fabricated activity or reviewed training rulings. No real match, referee, club, footage, or certification is implied.

The ordinary local application starts and remains `private`: an owner sees only their own contributions, with no other-owner text or aggregate counts. An isolated test database may enable `test-shared` for automated two-owner acceptance with synthetic owners and a test moderator. This requires a separate reviewed test-only Next runtime/entry with its own build directory, exact loopback origin, restricted test login, exact `_test` database, and migration-owned test enablement row. It must not weaken or bypass the ordinary `validateApplicationRuntime` rejection of test databases. The switch fails closed in the ordinary application database and production builds. A `moderated-local-pilot` may expose approved comments and eligible aggregates to other local owners only after the gates below and Nicolas's explicit decision on the concrete reviewed flow. Public or externally shared use requires RV-035 operational readiness and separate deployment authorization.

The shared projection exposes only approved, nonexpired author's allowlisted plain-text comment and aggregate rating/reaction counts. It exposes no owner ID, token, stable public profile, report detail, pending/hidden/removed text, or moderator actor. Other authors appear as `Participant`; the caller appears as `You`. IDs in URLs and request bodies never authorize mutation.

## Fixture identity and rating meaning

Repository-authored bilingual fixture source is canonical. An immutable PostgreSQL register stores `matchId`, version, canonical payload hash, and reviewed bilingual decision/context. The import compares compiled source hash to the register row. An interaction or shared projection fails closed for a missing or mismatched row. The read view offering an interaction displays that exact registered version and payload. The existing fixtures lack a fictional referee decision; add one and increment their versions before enabling ratings. The decision and context define the rating target. A rating is visibly an owner's opinion about that fictional decision, not RefView scoring of a training answer.

An optional rating is an integer from 1 to 5 with localized labels and abstention. One current rating exists per owner and exact fixture version. The owner may revise or clear it. A monotonic revision survives clearing as a non-content tombstone. An older fixture version cannot inherit a newer rating. Counts and distributions derive from eligible PostgreSQL rows, never from fixture examples or client increments.

## Comments, reactions, and reports

Comments are flat, text-only, and ask separately for an observation or needed evidence and an interpretation. Normalize NFC, CRLF/CR to LF, and trim surrounding Unicode whitespace; preserve interior whitespace, reject NUL and unpaired surrogates, then require both fields nonempty with a combined maximum of 1,500 Unicode code points. Render escaped plain text. No HTML, Markdown, uploads, embedded media, automatic links, replies, mentions, or caller-supplied display name. Owners can delete their own comments; editing is deferred. Removal clears both stored text fields while retaining non-content state/revision/event data until privacy deletion. Owners see their pending and removed state; other owners see only approved visible comments in the selected pilot. Approval concerns discussion suitability, not football correctness.

Each owner has one desired reaction per readable comment: `like`, `dislike`, or none. Switching and clearing are atomic and idempotent. Hidden, pending, removed, expired-author, or wrong-fixture comments cannot be reacted to or counted. An owner may report a readable comment with one reason from `abuse`, `privacy`, `spam`, or `other`, plus optional normalized plain-text detail of at most 500 Unicode code points under the same normalization/rejection rules. One report exists per reporter/comment; retries cannot inflate a count. Identity and detail are moderator-only. A report opens review but never directly hides text or grants moderator authority. Moderator transition reasons are `approved`, `reported-abuse`, `reported-privacy`, `reported-spam`, `operator-review`, and `owner-deletion`; required operator notes are bounded to 500 normalized code points and never shared.

## Owner, moderator, and database authority

The existing seven-day, nonrenewing anonymous cookie is the contributor owner. Bootstrap is an explicit same-origin action; feature mutations never create owners. Every Server Action validates application runtime, exact configured Origin, bounded input, and current owner. PostgreSQL resolves the token hash through `application_lock_owner`; Community mutations take its `exclusive` owner lock to serialize revisions, keys, and quotas, while reads use `share`. Caller-supplied owner IDs are rejected. The app role has no direct contributor-table access. Migration-owned narrow functions with pinned `pg_catalog` search paths enforce owner-scoped reads and writes, fixture version, and visibility again. A private read cannot return another owner's row.

Moderation has a distinct local database role and immutable role-to-principal mapping owned by the migration role. An interactive operator claim rotates its credential, records issuance evidence, and verifies its separate login; no real shared pilot precedes operator acceptance. Credentials stay outside Next environment and HTTP. A server-only CLI reads a bounded queue and invokes revision-checked, narrow transition procedures. Those procedures derive actor from authenticated `session_user` and mapping, verify role attributes and grants, and never accept caller-supplied actor. The app, Academy reviewer, and PUBLIC have no moderator grants. State transitions are `pending -> visible | removed`, `visible -> hidden | removed`, `hidden -> visible | removed`; `removed` is terminal. Each change appends an event with actor, expected/prior/next revision, reason, and database time. No user or report directly sets moderation state.

## Revisions, retries, locks, and quotas

Use immutable fixture rows, owner ratings/comments/reactions/reports, moderation events, and bounded receipts/counters with concrete foreign keys and composite fixture-version constraints. The first persistence migration includes structural comment rows/states and readable-target checks so reactions have a real FK; public comment creation and moderation follow in RV-033. Desired-state mutations require an expected revision and owner-scoped request key bound to a canonical payload hash covering every typed argument. The same key with different payload fails. An exact retry returns a prior non-content result only while its recorded target revision is current and after revalidating owner and target visibility; a later target revision makes it stale, never a new write. A deleted/expired/invisible target returns a generic unavailable result without prior text. Cleared rating/reaction state retains a monotonic revision. A comment-creation retry after deletion cannot recreate or return removed text. Receipts expire at seven days or owner/target deletion/expiry, whichever is first; non-content key tombstones persist for the remaining owner lifetime so an old retry cannot become a new creation. A deliberate new operation uses a new key. At most 500 accepted request keys may be retained per owner per rolling twenty-four hours, including successful no-ops and clears; deletion and self-delete never require a new key. This separate storage bound does not change action quotas.

Lock acting owner first, then target comment, receipt, and quota counter. Moderation locks target comment before events. For reaction/report, check target-author expiry with a nonlocking snapshot after the comment lock, then recheck under the locked target before commit. Author deletion locks and removes that comment before completing. Never take the author's owner lock after a target comment lock. Community comment deletion, self-delete, and purge take one migration-owned privacy-deletion serialization lock after owner lock and before child deletion; ordinary reactions/reports/moderation never take it. Multi-owner paths use consistent owner-ID order and avoid holding arbitrary owner locks while waiting on a comment. Test simultaneous cross-owner self-deletions, deletion, expiry, moderation, and self-delete races. Exact retries never consume new quota.

Limits use PostgreSQL wall time sampled after the owner and counter locks, with rolling half-open windows `(now - duration, now]`; an event exactly at the lower boundary is excluded. Per owner: five new comments per sixty minutes and twenty per twenty-four hours; twenty new reports per twenty-four hours; sixty combined rating and reaction creates or switches per sixty seconds. Maximum shared page size is fifty. Enforce atomically with the mutation under concurrency. Only committed new comments/reports or committed rating/reaction creates and switches consume quota. No-ops, exact retries, stale/conflicting requests, rejected validation, clears, deletes, and self-delete do not. Deletes remain available at limits. These limits apply to a session, not a person; cookie reset can create another owner. No IP tracking or fingerprinting is introduced. A local pilot is not evidence of internet-scale abuse resistance.

## Retention and deletion

Expired owners immediately lose read/write access. Their shared comments and ratings disappear from projections before physical purge; their reactions and reports cease contributing. Self-delete/purge transactionally removes an owner's ratings, comments, reactions, reports, receipts, counters, and linked moderation events. Moderation events are append-only while their comment exists; privacy deletion is the sole deletion path and cascades atomically through a migration-owned procedure, never direct app privileges. A removed but retained comment keeps non-content event/receipt data until owner deletion or expiry. Deleting a comment removes its reactions, reports, and linked moderation records. Canonical fictional fixtures remain. Retained operational aggregates contain no text, owner ID, comment ID, or recoverable link. Browser device preferences follow their separate reset. Before public use, set an actual purge schedule, monitor failures, publish a truthful retention statement, and name an abuse-response operator.
