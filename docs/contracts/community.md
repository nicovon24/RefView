## Fictional Community read contract v1

### Scope

This contract permits read-only local demonstration content before owner identity, rating, comment, report, and moderation contracts are complete. It does not authorize user-generated content or public release.

### Fixture identity

- Each match has an immutable stable `matchId`, authored competition name, date, two distinct fictional team names, and bilingual description.
- At least two matches have different identities and distinct detail paths. A detail path is resolved by `matchId`; an unknown ID returns a not-found state.
- Every list card and detail page explicitly says the match and any displayed observations are fictional demonstration content. No real club, referee, player, competition, or footage is implied.
- The fixture register records author, version, and provenance of any external assets. The first read slice uses text and repository-authored styling only, so it requires no external visual asset.

### Read boundary

- A fixture may describe a fictional match incident as a discussion prompt, but must not present a reviewed training ruling or fabricated community ratings/comments as live activity.
- Rating distributions, comment counts, and reactions are omitted until real owner-bound persistence exists. A future demo fixture distribution may appear only with an explicit fixture label.
- Read routes do not accept mutations, collect identity, or reveal an anonymous-session token.
- Localized URLs preserve the selected language for list and detail navigation.

### Review gate

Before owner-bound interaction, freeze `RV-030` covering anonymous ownership, uniqueness, moderation/reporting, rate limits, visibility, retention, and deletion. Its persistence implementation must be verified against PostgreSQL and reviewed for privacy and authorization. Read-only fixture acceptance does not imply that gate has passed.

<a id="community-interactions-v1"></a>
## Fictional Community interactions v1

### Scope and exposure

Two repository-authored match fixtures are explicitly fictional. Their names, teams, dates, incidents, and referee decisions are invented. Ratings and comments are real owner contributions about that fiction, never fabricated activity or reviewed training rulings. No real match, referee, club, footage, or certification is implied.

The ordinary local application starts and remains `private`: an owner sees only their own contributions, with no other-owner text or aggregate counts. An isolated test database may enable `test-shared` for automated two-owner acceptance with synthetic owners and a test moderator. This requires a separate reviewed test-only Next runtime/entry with its own build directory, exact loopback origin, restricted test login, exact `_test` database, and migration-owned test enablement row. It must not weaken or bypass the ordinary `validateApplicationRuntime` rejection of test databases. The switch fails closed in the ordinary application database and production builds. A `moderated-local-pilot` may expose approved comments and eligible aggregates to other local owners only after the gates below and Nicolas's explicit decision on the concrete reviewed flow. Public or externally shared use requires RV-035 operational readiness and separate deployment authorization.

The shared projection exposes only approved, nonexpired author's allowlisted plain-text comment and aggregate rating/reaction counts. It exposes no owner ID, token, stable public profile, report detail, pending/hidden/removed text, or moderator actor. Other authors appear as `Participant`; the caller appears as `You`. IDs in URLs and request bodies never authorize mutation.

### Fixture identity and rating meaning

Repository-authored bilingual fixture source is canonical. An immutable PostgreSQL register stores `matchId`, version, canonical payload hash, and reviewed bilingual decision/context. The import compares compiled source hash to the register row. An interaction or shared projection fails closed for a missing or mismatched row. The read view offering an interaction displays that exact registered version and payload. The existing fixtures lack a fictional referee decision; add one and increment their versions before enabling ratings. The decision and context define the rating target. A rating is visibly an owner's opinion about that fictional decision, not RefView scoring of a training answer.

An optional rating is an integer from 1 to 5 with localized labels and abstention. One current rating exists per owner and exact fixture version. The owner may revise or clear it. A monotonic revision survives clearing as a non-content tombstone. An older fixture version cannot inherit a newer rating. Counts and distributions derive from eligible PostgreSQL rows, never from fixture examples or client increments.

### Comments, reactions, and reports

Comments are flat, text-only, and ask separately for an observation or needed evidence and an interpretation. Normalize NFC, CRLF/CR to LF, and trim surrounding Unicode whitespace; preserve interior whitespace, reject NUL and unpaired surrogates, then require both fields nonempty with a combined maximum of 1,500 Unicode code points. Render escaped plain text. No HTML, Markdown, uploads, embedded media, automatic links, replies, mentions, or caller-supplied display name. Owners can delete their own comments; editing is deferred. Removal clears both stored text fields while retaining non-content state/revision/event data until privacy deletion. Owners see their pending and removed state; other owners see only approved visible comments in the selected pilot. Approval concerns discussion suitability, not football correctness.

Each owner has one desired reaction per readable comment: `like`, `dislike`, or none. Switching and clearing are atomic and idempotent. Hidden, pending, removed, expired-author, or wrong-fixture comments cannot be reacted to or counted. An owner may report a readable comment with one reason from `abuse`, `privacy`, `spam`, or `other`, plus optional normalized plain-text detail of at most 500 Unicode code points under the same normalization/rejection rules. One report exists per reporter/comment; retries cannot inflate a count. Identity and detail are moderator-only. A report opens review but never directly hides text or grants moderator authority. Moderator transition reasons are `approved`, `reported-abuse`, `reported-privacy`, `reported-spam`, `operator-review`, and `owner-deletion`; required operator notes are bounded to 500 normalized code points and never shared.

### Owner, moderator, and database authority

The existing seven-day, nonrenewing anonymous cookie is the contributor owner. Bootstrap is an explicit same-origin action; feature mutations never create owners. Every Server Action validates application runtime, exact configured Origin, bounded input, and current owner. PostgreSQL resolves the token hash through `application_lock_owner`; Community mutations take its `exclusive` owner lock to serialize revisions, keys, and quotas, while reads use `share`. Caller-supplied owner IDs are rejected. The app role has no direct contributor-table access. Migration-owned narrow functions with pinned `pg_catalog` search paths enforce owner-scoped reads and writes, fixture version, and visibility again. A private read cannot return another owner's row.

Moderation has a distinct local database role and immutable role-to-principal mapping owned by the migration role. An interactive operator claim rotates its credential, records issuance evidence, and verifies its separate login; no real shared pilot precedes operator acceptance. Credentials stay outside Next environment and HTTP. A server-only CLI reads a bounded queue and invokes revision-checked, narrow transition procedures. Those procedures derive actor from authenticated `session_user` and mapping, verify role attributes and grants, and never accept caller-supplied actor. The app, Academy reviewer, and PUBLIC have no moderator grants. State transitions are `pending -> visible | removed`, `visible -> hidden | removed`, `hidden -> visible | removed`; `removed` is terminal. Each change appends an event with actor, expected/prior/next revision, reason, and database time. No user or report directly sets moderation state.

### Revisions, retries, locks, and quotas

Use immutable fixture rows, owner ratings/comments/reactions/reports, moderation events, and bounded receipts/counters with concrete foreign keys and composite fixture-version constraints. The first persistence migration includes structural comment rows/states and readable-target checks so reactions have a real FK; public comment creation and moderation follow in RV-033. Desired-state mutations require an expected revision and owner-scoped request key bound to a canonical payload hash covering every typed argument. The same key with different payload fails. An exact retry returns a prior non-content result only while its recorded target revision is current and after revalidating owner and target visibility; a later target revision makes it stale, never a new write. A deleted/expired/invisible target returns a generic unavailable result without prior text. Cleared rating/reaction state retains a monotonic revision. A comment-creation retry after deletion cannot recreate or return removed text. Receipts expire at seven days or owner/target deletion/expiry, whichever is first; non-content key tombstones persist for the remaining owner lifetime so an old retry cannot become a new creation. A deliberate new operation uses a new key. At most 500 accepted request keys may be retained per owner per rolling twenty-four hours, including successful no-ops and clears; deletion and self-delete never require a new key. This separate storage bound does not change action quotas.

Lock acting owner first, then target comment, receipt, and quota counter. Moderation locks target comment before events. For reaction/report, check target-author expiry with a nonlocking snapshot after the comment lock, then recheck under the locked target before commit. Author deletion locks and removes that comment before completing. Never take the author's owner lock after a target comment lock. Community comment deletion, self-delete, and purge take one migration-owned privacy-deletion serialization lock after owner lock and before child deletion; ordinary reactions/reports/moderation never take it. Multi-owner paths use consistent owner-ID order and avoid holding arbitrary owner locks while waiting on a comment. Test simultaneous cross-owner self-deletions, deletion, expiry, moderation, and self-delete races. Exact retries never consume new quota.

Limits use PostgreSQL wall time sampled after the owner and counter locks, with rolling half-open windows `(now - duration, now]`; an event exactly at the lower boundary is excluded. Per owner: five new comments per sixty minutes and twenty per twenty-four hours; twenty new reports per twenty-four hours; sixty combined rating and reaction creates or switches per sixty seconds. Maximum shared page size is fifty. Enforce atomically with the mutation under concurrency. Only committed new comments/reports or committed rating/reaction creates and switches consume quota. No-ops, exact retries, stale/conflicting requests, rejected validation, clears, deletes, and self-delete do not. Deletes remain available at limits. These limits apply to a session, not a person; cookie reset can create another owner. No IP tracking or fingerprinting is introduced. A local pilot is not evidence of internet-scale abuse resistance.

### Retention and deletion

Expired owners immediately lose read/write access. Their shared comments and ratings disappear from projections before physical purge; their reactions and reports cease contributing. Self-delete/purge transactionally removes an owner's ratings, comments, reactions, reports, receipts, counters, and linked moderation events. Moderation events are append-only while their comment exists; privacy deletion is the sole deletion path and cascades atomically through a migration-owned procedure, never direct app privileges. A removed but retained comment keeps non-content event/receipt data until owner deletion or expiry. Deleting a comment removes its reactions, reports, and linked moderation records. Canonical fictional fixtures remain. Retained operational aggregates contain no text, owner ID, comment ID, or recoverable link. Browser device preferences follow their separate reset. Before public use, set an actual purge schedule, monitor failures, publish a truthful retention statement, and name an abuse-response operator.

### Verification and exposure gates

TDD against separate restricted PostgreSQL logins covers fixture hash mismatch, rating uniqueness/revision/retry/distribution, normalization/script payloads, reaction switch/clear/visibility, report idempotency, moderator-only transitions, concurrent quotas, lock order, expiry before purge, owner deletion rollback, and private DTO isolation. Playwright CLI uses two browser profiles against the isolated `test-shared` database: author posts, test moderator approves, another owner reads/reacts/reports, moderator hides/removes, and deletion/expiry clears visibility/counts. Verify both locales, keyboard/mobile, RSC/HTML/action privacy, and fiction/opinion labels. Independent authority, privacy, schema, and final milestone reviews are required.

The ordinary application remains private until Nicolas reviews the actual UI, moderator operation, seven-day deletion behavior, and limits, then explicitly selects `moderated-local-pilot`. A private demonstration does not satisfy COM-REQ-005 or full RV-034 acceptance. RV-035 gates public/external exposure and does not itself authorize deployment.

Read-only Astra/high advice informed the initial draft on 2026-11-04 (runtime-confirmed identity unconfirmed). Independent Sol/high review returned PASS after the first corrections. A later read-only Astra/high implementation design identified the separate test runtime, bounded key storage, normalization, and deletion-lock details incorporated above. Focused independent Sol/high re-review returned PASS for these additions; runtime-confirmed identity remained unconfirmed.

## Social Community authority contract v1

This contract defines the functional Community journey under SOC-01. The scoped local match/post, reaction, report, and moderation path described below is active; uploads, ratings, comments, and deployment remain outside this slice. The fictional preview cards and detail routes were removed from the public app at Nicolas's request; fixture records remain separate from contributor posts. Task status lives only in `docs/specs/tasks.md`.

### Immediate local sharing decision (2026-11-06)

Nicolas subsequently requested that real account-owned play posts and agreement/disagreement reactions be visible to everyone visiting his local RefView app. He chose immediate publication over manual preapproval. For this scoped local slice, an authenticated contributor's valid complete match and post become `visible` on creation with database-derived author, timestamp, and publication state. Signed-out visitors may read the shared feed; only eligible authenticated accounts may create, react, report, or remove their own content. Repository-authored fictional fixtures never become contributor posts or acquire illustrative counts in the shared feed. This decision supersedes the pending-on-create and manual preapproval wording below for the scoped local match/post path only. It does not authorize deployment, network exposure, uploaded media, ratings, comments, or editing visible text.

Immediate sharing requires database-enforced creation and reaction limits, one current reaction per account/post with change/removal, owner removal, reporting, a separately authorized moderator hide/remove path, stable match ancestry, and one visibility predicate for feed, direct reads, actions, and counts. Reports never hide content automatically. Reactions express opinions, not football correctness. Hidden/removed matches suppress descendant posts and reactions. Account deletion removes authored and reacted content under the shared social gate. Existing pending records remain private until a separate owner action or migration policy explicitly publishes them; a deployment cannot infer approval from this local decision. After restricted PostgreSQL, bilingual two-account browser acceptance, and independent authority/privacy review, Nicolas authorized local activation on 2026-11-06; `SocialExposure.mode` in local `refview` is `local_shared`.

For the immediate local slice, an exact or reversed-team identity that already has a visible match resolves to that match as the conversation grouping, with the returned ID bound to the operation receipt. A private or hidden matching record returns a generic unavailable outcome; it is never silently published or reparented. New matches with no such conflict are created visible in shared mode. Existing pending rows may not be made visible by a moderator transition unless an owner-authorized publication path is explicitly added later.

### Identity and content meaning

- A contributor must have a valid RefView account session. PostgreSQL derives the account from that session; no request may choose an author, account, owner bucket, moderator, or publication state. The seven-day anonymous owner and the existing `Community*` fixture tables cannot author `Social*` content.
- Public attribution uses an account-scoped, opaque Community profile and a contributor-chosen display name. Google name, email, picture, subject, tokens, session IDs, and learning-data owner IDs are never public profile fields. A role such as fan or referee and a claim of having watched a match are self-declared, not credentials.
- User submissions are opinions. Agreement and disagreement express a reader's view of a post or comment; they do not judge the football facts. A referee rating concerns one named referee's performance in one match, not a global referee score or a RefView training result. Repository examples retain an explicit fictional/source label and never acquire invented real-user activity.

### Records and constraints

The additive social schema uses distinct `SocialContributor`, `SocialMatch`, `SocialPlayPost`, `SocialRefereeAssignment`, `SocialMatchRating`, `SocialDiscussion`, `SocialComment`, `SocialReaction`, `SocialReport`, `SocialModerationEvent`, `SocialExposure`, and `SocialLink` records as their functions are enabled. SOC-02 begins with private contributor, match, play-post, exposure, and operation-key records; SOC-03 adds ratings, discussions, reactions, reports, moderation, and links before those functions are exposed. A later reviewed media contract may add `SocialAttachment`. Existing fixture tables and IDs remain untouched.

- Every contributor record has a database-derived account ID and a stable match ancestry. A play post belongs to one match. A referee assignment belongs to one match and requires a bounded display name before any rating can be submitted, never a global referee identity. A rating belongs to one assignment and one account, with a unique active rating per pair, an integer score from 1 to 5, a required reason, and an explicit self-declared watched flag. Unnamed assignments remain unrated drafts.
- A discussion has exactly one post or match-rating root, enforced by concrete foreign keys and a one-of check. A comment belongs to that discussion. A reply has one parent comment in the same discussion and match; composite ancestry constraints and mutation checks reject cross-match, cross-discussion, and cyclic references. Display replies with one visual indentation level, regardless of reply depth. Reactions target exactly one post or comment, with one current agree/disagree choice per account and target. Counts derive from eligible stored rows.
- Use immutable IDs, database timestamps, monotonic revisions, and explicit state transitions. Updates and deletions use expected revisions. A replayed operation cannot create a second record, apply to a new revision, or resurrect removed content. A stored receipt contains no content or private identity in public projections.
- Contributor publication requires both teams and a known match date; competition may explicitly be unspecified. Incomplete identity may remain an owner-private draft. Match identity uses NFC, trimmed whitespace, and case-normalized competition, ordered teams, and match date. A complete exact key is unique among active canonical matches; a reversed team order is a likely duplicate. An exact duplicate returns the readable match or a generic unavailable outcome when its existence is private. A likely duplicate is held for review. A moderator may mark an unpublished match as duplicate and point to an eligible canonical match; published discussions are never automatically merged or reparented.

### Visibility and moderation

`draft`, `pending`, `visible`, `hidden`, and `removed` are distinct states. The author can read and revise their own draft or pending item; other contributors see only visible items. A submitted match, post, rating, comment, link, and future attachment begins pending. An authorized moderator can transition `pending -> visible | removed`, `visible -> hidden | removed`, or `hidden -> visible | removed`, with an append-only event containing actor, prior and next state, revision, reason, and database time. Removed is terminal. A content-changing edit of a visible item increments revision and returns it to pending before the new bytes are eligible for any shared projection.

A contributor may report a currently readable match, post, rating, comment, or link once per account and exact target; reports on inaccessible or removed targets return a generic unavailable result. A report records one bounded reason (`abuse`, `privacy`, `spam`, or `other`) and optional bounded plain-text detail, with a private reporter/target unique key and idempotent exact retry. It opens a moderator-only queue and never changes visibility by itself. Reports and moderator notes are absent from public DTOs and counts. A moderator transition records the target's expected revision and may cite report IDs, but the moderator independently chooses the transition and reason; a reporter never receives authority over the target.

Eligibility is the conjunction of the item's state, all ancestor states including reply ancestors, author account eligibility, link/media state, and the current exposure mode. The same predicate governs feeds, direct routes, Server Actions, search, counts, previews, and media responses. A hidden or removed match hides every descendant; a hidden post or rating hides its discussion; a hidden comment hides its replies and reactions. Counts and cursor pages must be recomputed from eligible rows, not cached client totals. The private mode shows only the caller's own contribution graph and no other-author text or aggregate. Moderator inspection is a separate allowlisted projection. A missing or unauthorized direct ID returns a generic unavailable response without disclosing whether it exists. Shared pages cap at 50 and order by `(publishedAt DESC, id DESC)` after applying visibility.

The scoped match/post path is locally shared after the acceptance evidence in `../features/03-community/review.md#20-community-social` and Nicolas's explicit decision. An isolated acceptance runtime used two synthetic accounts; restricted moderation was checked in `refview_test`. The broader SOC-04 journey, including uploads, ratings, comments, direct post routes, and full media review, remains open. This local activation does not authorize deployment or network exposure.

### Account and database authority

Use additive PostgreSQL migrations owned by the restricted migration role. The application role receives narrow function grants, not direct table writes. Mutations validate exact same-origin requests, bounded input, the current account session, expected revision, and database authorization. Moderator credentials and functions are separate from the application and Academy reviewer roles; no HTTP route accepts a moderator credential. Account-wide uniqueness and quotas use the auth account ID, not an attached anonymous bucket. For the initial local implementation, acquire the acting account lock, then one shared social mutation/deletion gate, then target rows in stable match-to-descendant order, then request-key and quota rows. Account deletion takes its account lock, then the same social gate, then attached owner roots in stable ID order, then social and learning descendants. A moderator transition establishes its separate moderator identity, takes the social gate, then locks target ancestry; it never takes a contributor account lock after the gate. Every procedure touching social descendants uses this gate. The gate deliberately limits concurrency. Never take another contributor's account lock after locking content.

Issue bounded, server-originated operation keys with a 24-hour lifetime. An unknown, expired, or reused key with different canonical payload fails. Keep a consumed non-content key until expiry, including after target deletion, so retries cannot recreate content. Limit issuance to 500 keys per account per rolling 24 hours, and add separate reviewed creation/reaction/report limits before shared use. Owner deletion and self-removal do not require a fresh key. No browser-supplied count, author, moderation state, or target ancestry is authoritative.

### Links, files, and rights

The first functional media path is a bounded HTTPS outbound link: allow only `https`, limit URL length and stored display text, reject credentials and local/private destinations, render an explicit external destination, and never server-fetch, proxy, auto-embed, or treat it as match evidence. Store the original submitted URL only in a private record; expose a validated display URL. Link changes return the containing item to pending.

Photo and file upload is explicitly closed in v1. The initial upload proposal, requiring a separate review before implementation, allows JPEG, PNG, WebP, and PDF only, at most 10 MiB each and three per parent; it rejects SVG, HTML, executables, archives, and video. A processing worker must verify bytes and decoded size, scan, strip image metadata, re-encode images, reject active or encrypted PDFs, and quarantine originals. Each attachment binds to one account and one immutable post or comment parent, with source and rights attestation, processing state, moderation state, and private storage key. Authorized delivery rechecks all ancestors and uses short-lived access; no permanent public object URL survives hiding. An outbox handles physical deletion after transactional delivery denial. The storage backend, worker, precise pixel/page limits, failure recovery, retention, and deletion service level must be fixed in a separate reviewed media contract before enabling uploads. Native video upload is outside v1. The earlier generated fictional demonstration images were removed from the public app and its assets; they were never contributor uploads or match footage.

### Deletion and retention

Account deletion must atomically revoke sessions and remove the account's social posts, matches, ratings, comments, reactions, reports, links, moderation references, and derived counts, including cross-author descendants, before deleting the auth user. Deleting an owned match removes its entire discussion subtree; deleting a post or rating removes its discussion; deleting a comment removes its reply subtree. The confirmation copy must explain that a match or post owner can thereby remove other people's replies. This choice can be revised only through a separate ownership-transfer design. No text is silently reattributed. The operation requires an additive revision of `application_delete_account`; the existing procedure only deletes attached learning-data buckets and auth records. User removal clears content from all shared projections immediately. Future external files require an authorization denial and transactionally recorded deletion outbox; physical blob deletion occurs afterward with retry and monitoring, never as a claimed PostgreSQL transaction. Retention is until account deletion, subject to a published operational policy before any shared release.

Before SOC-02, independently review the concrete schema, lock order, account-deletion revision, duplicate handling, projection allowlists, and moderator role. The proposed upload path must receive its separate media review before any upload implementation; links alone may be delivered first. Before each additional shared surface under SOC-04, prove its restricted PostgreSQL ownership, retries, uniqueness, races, ancestor hiding, direct-route privacy, account deletion, media denial, and two-account/moderator ES/EN flows. The scoped local match/post path has its own evidence in the feature review. No broader milestone status advances to verified based on this contract alone.
